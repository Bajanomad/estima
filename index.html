How we judge

Every assessment is built in the same order:

  • The verified floor first. What the record actually establishes, checked by Contraste, our verification publication, and by primary sources.
  • Then the rest, weighed. What leakers and tracked outside sources are saying, tiered by their own track records. What officials are publicly saying, read as evidence of what the speaker wants believed. What other think tanks and analysts are saying.
  • Then our call, with numbers. The most probable explanation, stated as judgment in controlled estimative words, with likelihood and confidence kept separate, and a forecast of where it is heading.

The full rules are published on the Method page, before the first resolution, so they cannot be written around the results.

The verification floor

ESTIMA judges. Contraste proves. Contraste checks claims against the record and publishes only what the evidence supports, under the labels PROVEN, INFERENCE, and UNVERIFIED. Every ESTIMA assessment links down to the Contraste entries and primary evidence its factual premises rest on, so a reader can re-derive the judgment or reject it on the evidence. The wall between the two houses is absolute: Contraste never judges, and ESTIMA never dresses judgment as proof.

What we will not do

  • No recommendations, ever. We publish what we think is really going on and where it is heading. Governments, companies, and the public do with that what they will.
  • Nothing sealed is ever edited. Resolutions and corrections are appended, dated, and explained.
  • Judgment is never dressed as proof, and a forecast is never quietly dropped. A missed deadline stays on the record as a miss.

Tips and corrections

Two separate doors, both pre-moderated: a tips desk for leads that feed our source registry, and a corrections desk for challenges to anything we have published. Nothing submitted becomes evidence by being submitted. See Tips and Corrections.

About the team

ESTIMA is produced by one human and a team of AI agents working from named desks: research, language, social signal, deep reasoning, long context, multimodal, independent assessment, and coordination.
